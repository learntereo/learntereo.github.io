## Saying what someone should do

Use *me* and then the doing word:

- *Me haere koe ki te kura*: you should go to school
- *Me whakarongo mai koe*: you should listen

## Saying what someone should not do

Use *kaua e* and then the doing word:

- *Kaua e kōrero*: do not talk
- *Kaua e oma*: do not run

## Wishes and encouragement

*Kia* is used for how you hope someone will be:

- *Kia tūpato*: be careful
- *Kia kaha*: be strong

## Simple orders

A plain order can be just the doing word, like *Tatari* (wait). Add *koa* to make it polite: *Tēnā koa tatari mai* means "Please wait".

## Watch out

After *kaua*, always put *e* before the doing word. *Kia* has other uses too, so for now remember it with *kia tūpato* and *kia kaha*.
