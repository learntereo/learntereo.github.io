## Saying something hurts

Use *Kei te mamae*, then the body part with "my":

- *Kei te mamae tōku upoko*: my head hurts
- *Kei te mamae ōku waewae*: my legs hurt

## My, for one and for many

Body parts take the O forms of "my":

- *tōku*: my (one thing)
- *ōku*: my (more than one thing)

So you say *tōku puku* for one stomach and *ōku ringa* for your hands.

## Counting body parts

*E rua ōku karu* means "I have two eyes". It uses the same counting pattern as numbers: *E*, a number, then the thing.

## Watch out

Do not use *taku* or *aku* for body parts. You will learn why in the unit on mine and yours.
