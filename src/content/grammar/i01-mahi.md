## Two ways to say "is doing"

You already know *Kei te*. There is a second pattern for an action that is going on right now: *E*, the doing word, then *ana*.

- *E kai ana te kurī*: the dog is eating
- *E moe ana te pēpi*: the baby is sleeping
- *E waiata ana ngā tamariki*: the children are singing

## Putting it together

The doing word sits between *E* and *ana*. Then comes who is doing it. You can swap in *Kei te* with the same meaning: *Kei te pānui ahau i te pukapuka* means "I am reading the book".

## Watch out

Use *E* and *ana* as a pair, with the doing word between them. For an action happening now, *Kei te* and *E ... ana* mean the same thing, so you can choose either. *I* still marks the thing being acted on after *Kei te*: *Kei te pānui ahau i te pukapuka*.
